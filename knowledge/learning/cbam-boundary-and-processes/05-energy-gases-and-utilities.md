# Module 5 — Energy, Gases, Utilities and Meters

## 1. Fuels (combustion)

| Fuel | Typical unit | What to ask | Document |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coking coal | t | mass, source, NCV | purchase invoice, CoA |
| Coke / coke breeze | t | mass, use | invoice, weighbridge |
| PCI coal | t | injection rate | invoice, logs |
| Natural gas (NG) | SCM / Nm³ | volume, NCV | utility bill, meter |
| Diesel | L | litres, use | invoices, logs |
| Fuel oil | L / t | mass/volume, NCV | invoices, CoA |
| LPG | kg / L | mass, use | invoices |

## 2. Gases — the part most often missed

| Gas | Where it comes from | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **BFG** (blast-furnace gas) | blast furnace | low-calorific fuel; CO/CO₂/H₂/N₂; used in stoves/boilers/power |
| **BOFG** (BOF gas) | BOF converter | CO-rich; recovered/used/flared |
| **COG** (coke-oven gas) | coke oven | H₂/CH₄/CO; used as fuel |
| **NG** | purchased | methane; combustion |
| **H₂** | purchased / DRI | reduction or fuel |

Ask:
- Is the gas **metered**, and where does it go (stove, boiler, power plant, flare)?
- Is it **recovered, flared, vented, or exported**?
- What is the **transfer/attribution** between processes?

> Gas transfers are a classic source of double-counting or omission. Record the source, destination
> and meter.

## 3. Reduction agents

| Agent | Route | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Coke | BF | reductant + fuel; coke rate |
| Coal / PCI | BF | partial replacement of coke |
| Natural gas | DRI / BF | reductant + fuel |
| H₂ | DRI | low-carbon reductant |

Ask for **rates** (kg/t hot metal) and the **qualifying parameters** (see Module 6).

## 4. On-site generation vs imported/exported

| Source | What to ask |
|---|---|
| **Captive power plant** | fuel type, fuel logs, MWh generated, own use vs export |
| **Waste-heat recovery** | heat source, MWh/GJ, use |
| **Solar/wind** | capacity, MWh, PPA/ownership |
| **Imported electricity** | meter, bills, MWh, grid factor |
| **Imported steam/heat** | supplier, GJ/t, meter, contract |
| **Exported steam/heat/electricity** | destination, meter, agreement |

Distinguish **generation** (fuel emissions at the plant) from **purchase** (grid factor) and
**export** (allocation/credit rules).

## 5. The meter map

For every measured stream:

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Meter ID | traceability |
| Stream | what it measures |
| Location | where in the plant |
| Unit | MWh, Nm³, t, GJ |
| Range / class | accuracy |
| Calibration date | verifier evidence |
| Reading frequency | monthly/daily/real-time |
| Owner | who maintains it |
| Data system | where the reading lives |

**Metrology facts:** electricity meters (MID), weighbridges (OIML), lab analysis (ISO/IEC 17025).
Ask for **calibration certificates** — an expired certificate is a verifier flag.

## 6. Units you will meet

| Quantity | Units | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | kWh, MWh, MJ, GJ, TJ | 1 MWh = 3.6 GJ |
| Gas volume | SCM, Nm³, m³ | **different bases** — do not assume 1:1 |
| Mass | kg, t | use net mass |
| Emissions | kgCO₂, tCO₂, tCO₂e | state GWP version |
| Heat | GJ, t steam, °C | record pressure/temperature |

## 7. What to ask (facts)

- Fuel invoices and meter logs for the period.
- Gas meters and destinations; flaring/venting records.
- Reduction-agent rates (kg/t).
- Generation meters and fuel logs; import/export contracts.
- Meter list with calibration certificates.
- The units and reading frequency for each stream.

## 8. Common mistakes

- Missing BFG/BOFG/COG.
- Treating SCM and Nm³ as the same.
- Counting exported electricity as consumed.
- Ignoring calibration.
- Using a nameplate capacity instead of a meter reading.
