# Module 2 — The Plant as a System; Entity vs Installation

## 1. The plant as a system

Every plant is **inputs → processes → outputs**:

```
fuels, ores, scrap, electricity, water
              │
              ▼
   [ process step ] → [ process step ] → … → finished good
              │                │
              ▼                ▼
        by-products, gases, waste, heat
```

To map it, you need the **process flow** and the **site layout**. Both are facts.

## 2. Process flow vs site layout

| Artefact | What it shows | Why we need it |
|---|---|---|
| **Process flow diagram** | steps, material/energy flows, gases | defines the system boundary and source streams |
| **Site layout / plot plan** | physical location of equipment, meters, fence line | separates the installation from neighbours and shared utilities |

A hand-drawn diagram is acceptable — it is a fact base, not a work of art.

## 3. Entity vs installation vs good

| Concept | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal entity** | the company | “ABC Steel Pvt Ltd” |
| **Installation** | the technical unit/site under one operator | the Rajkot plant |
| **Good** | the product/CN code | HRC, CN 7208 |
| **System boundary** | the processes included for the good | reheat + rolling + pickling for a re-roller |

The **installation** is the anchor. A company can have several installations; a site can host
several tenants.

## 4. Co-located tenants and shared utilities

Ask:
- Is any equipment or utility **shared** with another company on the same site?
- Who owns the **captive power plant**, the **steam line**, the **water treatment**, the
  **weighbridge**?
- Where is the **fence line**?
- Are there **co-located tenants**? What do they produce?
- Is electricity/heat **imported from** or **exported to** a neighbour?

Facts to request: site plan, lease/ownership map, utility contracts, meter ownership.

## 5. The fence line

The fence line is a useful physical proxy, but the boundary is **process-based**. Record:
- What is inside the fence.
- What crosses the fence (electricity, gas, steam, materials, waste).
- Who meters the crossing.

## 6. What to ask (facts)

- [ ] Process flow diagram (even hand-drawn).
- [ ] Site layout / plot plan with the fence line.
- [ ] List of process steps and what each does.
- [ ] Shared utilities and who owns/meters them.
- [ ] Co-located tenants and their products.
- [ ] Imported/exported electricity, heat, steam, gases.
- [ ] Existing monitoring plan or site drawings.

## 7. Worked example

A re-roller on an industrial estate:
- Fence line surrounds reheat furnace, rolling mill, pickling, coating.
- The captive power plant is **shared** with a neighbouring company → electricity is imported;
  record the meter and contract.
- HRC arrives by truck → precursor, not an internal process.
- Boundary = reheat → rolling → pickling → coating; ore/ironmaking are upstream via precursor SEE.

## 8. Common mistakes

- Drawing a boundary from a template instead of the plant.
- Including office loads or excluding a real process.
- Treating a shared utility as owned without checking the meter.
- Confusing the company with the installation.
