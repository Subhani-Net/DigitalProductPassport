# Module 1 — Facts, Not Conclusions

## 1. The distinction

| Conclusion (what people say) | Fact (what we can verify) |
|---|---|
| “Our plant is efficient.” | “The reheat furnace used 180,000 SCM of gas in 2026; bill + meter log.” |
| “We don’t have emissions from that.” | “Process step X has no fuel input; the flow diagram shows no burner.” |
| “We use best technology.” | “Route is BF-BOF; EAF share is 0%; coke rate is [value].” |
| “Our consultant has the data.” | “Here is the monitoring plan, dated [date], section [x].” |
| “Electricity is about 500 MWh.” | “528,400 kWh on the 2026 bills; meter ID [x]; monthly readings.” |

A conclusion may be true, but it cannot be **audited**. Facts have a **source, unit, period and
owner**.

## 2. Why conclusions are dangerous

- They hide **omissions** (“we don’t have that stream”).
- They mix **units and periods**.
- They cannot be traced to a verifier.
- They create **defaults** where actual data could have been used — costing the client money.
- They make the report **unreproducible**.

## 3. The “show me” method

For every claim, ask five things:

1. **What** exactly is it? (stream, process, meter, document)
2. **Where** is it? (which line, meter, page, section)
3. **When**? (period, date, frequency)
4. **What unit**? (MWh, Nm³, t, GJ)
5. **Who** records it, and **how** is it measured?

Then ask for the **artefact**: bill, meter log, diagram, invoice, certificate, screenshot.

## 4. Question patterns that produce facts

| Instead of… | Ask… |
|---|---|
| “Do you have emissions data?” | “Can you show me the fuel invoices and the meter readings for the period?” |
| “What is your boundary?” | “Can you mark on this diagram where the fence line is, and which utilities are shared?” |
| “Do you use coke?” | “Which reduction agents go into the furnace? Show me the purchase invoices.” |
| “Is your electricity green?” | “Do you have a PPA or Guarantees of Origin? Show the contract and the MWh.” |
| “How much do you produce?” | “Show me the production log by product and the sales invoices for the same period.” |

## 5. Red flags (dig deeper)

- No process diagram, or a diagram with no flows.
- “We estimate” or “roughly” without a method.
- Units change mid-conversation (kWh vs MWh, SCM vs Nm³, kg vs t).
- Missing months, or a period that does not match the reporting year.
- “The consultant has it” with no document.
- A stream that should exist for the route is absent (e.g., no BFG at a BF-BOF plant).
- Someone answers a question you did not ask.

## 6. Handling “I don’t know”

That is a valid fact. Record it:
- Mark the item **Unknown** in the source-stream inventory.
- Ask a targeted follow-up (“who would know?”; “is there a meter?”).
- Record it in the gap register with a fallback.
- Do not fill the gap with an assumption.

## 7. Exercise

Turn these into fact requests:
1. “Our rolling mill is quite efficient.”
2. “We buy some scrap.”
3. “We have a captive power plant.”
4. “Our emissions are lower than the default.”

**Model answers:** ask for energy per tonne and the bills; scrap purchase invoices and
weighbridge; the plant’s fuel logs and generation meter; the actual SEE calculation and its source
documents — not the claim.
