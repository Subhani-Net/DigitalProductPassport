# CBAM 101 — Self-Assessment

Answer first, then check the key. Aim for 16/20 or better before starting CBAM 201.

## Section A — Recall (1 point each)

1. What does CBAM stand for, and which Regulation establishes it?
2. In which period are importers financially obliged, and when did it start?
3. Name the six covered sectors.
4. What is the de-minimis import threshold?
5. Who is the legally liable party under CBAM?
6. Name the three CBAM Registry access classes/modules.
7. Which body issues the emissions verification report, and must it be accredited?
8. What is SEE, in what unit?
9. What are the two components of embedded emissions?
10. What is the date of the first certificate surrender?

## Section B — Understanding (2 points each)

11. Explain the relationship between CBAM and the ETS free-allocation phase-down.
12. Why does a "precursor" widen the scope of CBAM?
13. Why are default values commercially harmful, and what behaviour do they drive?
14. Trace the data flow from a non-EU installation to the importer's declaration.
15. Why does the importer need verified actual values rather than self-reported figures?
16. How does the carbon-price deduction work?

## Section C — Application (3 points each)

17. An EU firm imports 40 t/yr of steel. Is it in scope? Explain.
18. A supplier cannot provide monitored data. What happens to the importer's cost, and why?
19. A primary aluminium smelter wants to lower its CBAM SEE. Name two levers.
20. Whom does the Registry make liable if a data error is found in a declaration, and why?

## Answer key (abbreviated)

**A:** 1 Reg (EU) 2023/956 · 2 Definitive period, 1 Jan 2026 · 3 Cement, iron&steel, aluminium,
fertilisers, electricity, hydrogen · 4 ≤50 t/yr · 5 the EU importer (Authorised CBAM Declarant)
· 6 AMM, O3CI, DRMC · 7 an accredited verifier (must be accredited) · 8 Specific Embedded
Emissions, tCO₂e/t · 9 direct + indirect (plus precursors for complex goods) · 10 30 Sep 2027.

**B:** 11 CBAM mirrors the ETS; as free allowances shrink, the import charge grows in step
· 12 goods made from other covered goods carry their upstream emissions, so the chain is in
scope · 13 defaults are punitive/higher, so they push suppliers to produce verified actual data
· 14 operator→O3CI→verifier report→Registry→declarant retrieves via EORI→declaration
· 15 only an accredited verifier's report makes actual values usable; self-reported values
don't count · 16 carbon price already paid in the origin country is deducted if evidenced.

**C:** 17 Out of scope — below the 50 t/yr de-minimis · 18 cost rises: defaults are punitive,
so the importer pays more and is pushed to obtain actual data · 19 lower-carbon electricity /
PPAs + GOs; reduce anode-effect PFCs (frequency/duration); route/energy efficiency · 20 the
importer (declarant) — liability sits with them, which is why they must verify supplier data.
