# CBAM 201 — Self-Assessment

Answer first, then check the key. Aim for **18/22** or better before starting CBAM 301.

## Section A — Recall (1 point each)
1. Write the SEE formula for a complex good.
2. Name the three permitted direct-emission methods.
3. What is a "source stream"?
4. What is the "functional unit"?
5. Which implementing act sets definitive-phase default values?
6. Name the two registry modules for non-EU operators and EU declarants.
7. By what date is the first annual declaration + surrender due?
8. What is the CBAM factor for 2026, and its 2034 value?
9. What is the excess-emissions penalty per tonne?
10. Which gases dominate aluminium's footprint besides electricity?

## Section B — Understanding (2 points each)
11. Why does the precursor term usually dominate a complex good's SEE?
12. Why does a conservative default mark-up exist, and what behaviour does it drive?
13. Verification vs validation — explain the difference and when each applies.
14. Why is the annual declaration deadline "30 September", and what changed via the Omnibus?
15. What makes evidence "verifier-ready", and why is that commercially important?
16. Explain the quarterly minimum holding rule.

## Section C — Application (3 points each)
17. Given direct 1,850,000 t, indirect 90,000 t, activity 1,000,000 t, precursor
    contribution 120,000 t → compute SEE.
18. An EAF steel plant has verified data; a competitor uses defaults. Explain the cost gap
    drivers as the CBAM factor rises.
19. An aluminium smelter wants lower SEE. Give three concrete levers and rank them.
20. A verifier finds an uncalibrated gas meter affecting a source stream. What is the likely
    consequence and the fix?
21. For a galvanised strip maker, list the precursor data needed and its two possible sources.
22. Draft a 5-point verifier-ready dossier checklist for a BF-BOF mill.

## Answer key (abbreviated)
**A:** 1 `SEE=Σ(direct+indirect)/AL + Σ(Mᵢ×SEEᵢ)` · 2 calc-based, CEMS, mass balance ·
3 specific fuel/material/product giving rise to emissions (or carbon in mass balance) ·
4 reference unit (e.g. per tonne) · 5 IR (EU) 2025/2621 (benchmarks 2025/2620) · 6 O3CI & AMM ·
7 30 Sep 2027 · 8 2.5% (2026); 100% (2034) · 9 €100/tCO₂e · 10 PFCs (anode effects; CF₄/C₂F₆).

**B:** 11 because upstream steel/DRI carries most embodied carbon · 12 to discourage defaults;
pushes operators/suppliers to produce verified actuals · 13 verification = assurance of the
numbers (when actuals used); validation = checking the monitoring plan before use ·
14 aligns with certificate settlement; Omnibus moved it from 31 May · 15 evidence traceable,
recalc-able, sourced (lab certs, meter logs) — it decides whether actuals survive verification
(escaping the default penalty) · 16 from 2027 hold certificates covering ≥50% of YTD emissions
each quarter-end.

**C:** 17 SEE = (1,850,000+90,000)/1,000,000 + 120,000/1,000,000 = 2.06 tCO₂e/t ·
18 defaults carry a mark-up + reflect worse-than-actual performance; as CBAM factor rises
2.5%→100%, the per-tonne gap × quantity grows · 19 lower-carbon electricity (PPA/GO), reduce
anode-effect PFCs, efficiency/route; rank by size of contribution (electricity & PFC first) ·
20 the affected value may fall back to default (costlier); fix = recalibrate + evidence +
re-verify · 21 precursor = crude steel: mass Mᵢ + SEEᵢ; sources = upstream verified data or EC
default (IR 2025/2621) · 22 monitoring plan; activity data + source tickets/invoices; ISO 17025
lab factors; route params; calibration/uncertainty + mass-balance workings.
