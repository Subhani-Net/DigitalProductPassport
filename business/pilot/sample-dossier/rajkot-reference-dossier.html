A note from Veriflow

What this dossier is — and what it is not

Veriflow builds verifier-ready CBAM evidence for steel and aluminium exporters. We take the documents your plant already produces — electricity and fuel bills, purchase invoices, weighbridge tickets and mill certificates — and turn them into a carbon figure an accredited verifier can check, and your EU buyer can reuse across every order.

This dossier is a worked example on synthetic data for a typical Indian re-rolling mill. It is here to show you the standard we hold ourselves to: every number ties to a source, every line is flagged actual or default, and nothing is claimed that we cannot show.

One boundary, stated plainly: we prepare evidence for review; the verification stamp is issued by an accredited partner verifier. We say "verifier-ready, not verified" on purpose — because that independence is what protects you.

How to read this document

  • The executive summary gives you the answer at a glance.
  • Sections 1–4 establish what was measured and where it came from.
  • Section 5 shows the calculation; Section 6 is the audit trail behind it.
  • Sections 7–8 record the assumptions, the open items, and the next steps.
Synthetic and illustrative. No real client, supplier or verification body is represented. Figures use the EU certificate price of €75.36/t (Q1 2026) and may move quarterly.

On a 1,000 t order that is approximately €4,520 in avoided certificate cost for the buyer; on 10,000 t, approximately €45,200. The certificate saving accrues primarily to the EU importer — your buyer; you benefit through retained orders and the ability to defend, rather than discount, your price.

Why the number moves

  • The bars are a complex good: the purchased billets carry most of the carbon, so the upstream data is the biggest lever.
  • For iron & steel, only direct emissions are counted initially; electricity is shown separately (see Section 5).
  • The billet SEE is an actual value (synthetic verification reference) — no punitive default is substituted in this example.
Bottom line: a verifiable number is not a compliance chore — it is a pricing asset. This dossier is the evidence behind that number.
