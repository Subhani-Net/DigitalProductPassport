# Presentation & Customer Conversations Plan (the "Present" dimension)

Third dimension of the operating model — **Learn ∥ Implement ∥ Present**. See
`sprint-plan.md` §3.1/§3.2. Purpose: turn building into customer credibility and stop
build-to-avoid. **No milestone is done without the Present artifact** defined here.

Target: **all three segments** (exporter, importer/declarant, verifier) until each has a
presentable MVP.

## 1. Segments & pain hypotheses (solution-led, not probing)

| Segment | Pain hypothesis we lead with | What "good" looks like for them |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-EU exporter / installation** | "Verified actuals → EU importers don't penalise you with defaults; protects/win the order." | Market access + customer retention; lower-friction data requests |
| **EU importer / authorised declarant** | "Declaration-ready, verifier-ready data → lower certificate cost and audit risk." | Cheaper declarations; fewer verification surprises |
| **Accredited verifier** | "Evidence dossiers + recalculation → faster, more reliable audits." | Throughput + lower risk |

## 2. Conversation-ready checklist (M-A gate)

You can hold a credible conversation when you can explain, without notes:
1. What CBAM is (ETS mirror, why it exists).
2. Who is liable (importer/declarant) vs who provides data (non-EU operator).
3. Actual vs default values and the cost gap.
4. What "verified" means and who can verify (accredited verifier).
5. The timeline (definitive live; first surrender 30 Sep 2027).
6. What our tool does in that flow (data → calc → verifier-ready output → DPP).
7. The one ask: *"Would a pilot on your data be useful?"*

**Per-segment framing:** exporter → market access/retention; importer → cost + audit risk;
verifier → audit speed/quality.

## 3. Solution-led script (4 lines; swap the middle line per segment)

1. "I've built a working CBAM data → declaration pipeline for steel."
2. *[Segment pain hypothesis from §1]*
3. "10-minute walkthrough on a sample dataset; if useful, pilot on your data."
4. "Worth 10 minutes?"

**Do not** ask market-research questions ("can I interview you about your pain"). Show, then
let them react.

## 4. Signal rubric & log

**Strength:** **Strong** = asks price / asks to try / introduces a colleague / shares real data.
**Medium** = detailed questions, compares to current tool. **Weak** = polite interest, no follow-up.

**Signal log fields:** date · segment · company · contact/route · ask made · signal strength ·
next step · notes.
